How-to guide

How much concrete do I need?

A 10 by 10 foot patio slab, 4 inches thick, takes 1.36 cubic yards of concrete — 62 bags of 80 lb mix. The maths is simple: multiply length by width by thickness, then divide by 27 to get cubic yards. For fence post holes, work out one hole and multiply.

How the maths works

Slabs: length × width × thickness, divided by 27

A cubic yard is 27 cubic feet — a cube 3 feet on every side. So a slab 10 feet by 10 feet at 4 inches thick is 10 × 10 × (4 ÷ 12) = 33.33 cubic feet, and 33.33 ÷ 27 = 1.23 cubic yards. The 10% waste allowance takes it to 1.36 cubic yards.

Bags: divide by what one bag makes

Bag yields are printed on the bag and are the same across the big brands: an 80 lb bag makes 0.60 cubic feet (45 bags per yard), a 60 lb bag makes 0.45 cubic feet (60 per yard), and a 40 lb bag makes 0.30 cubic feet (90 per yard). So 36.67 cubic feet ÷ 0.60 = 61.1 — we round up to 62 bags.

Post holes: the cylinder formula

A round hole is a cylinder: π × radius² × depth. A 12-inch hole, 24 inches deep, is π × 0.5² × 2 = 1.57 cubic feet. That is 3 bags of 80 lb mix per hole. The fence calculator works out the concrete for a whole fence line at once.

Bags or ready-mix?

Once a job passes about one cubic yard — 45 bags of 80 lb mix — ready-mix delivered by truck is usually cheaper and far less work than mixing bags by hand. Bags make sense for small pours and post holes; anything bigger, call the truck.

Pouring temperatures

Concrete pours best between 50 and 90°F. Below 40°F it sets very slowly and can be permanently damaged if it freezes; above 95°F it sets too fast, loses strength, and is outside the industry standard. The concrete calculator page has the full guide to concrete types and pouring temperatures.
