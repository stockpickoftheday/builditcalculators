How the maths works

Posts: length divided by spacing, plus one

Eight feet on center is the standard — it matches 8 foot rail stock with no waste, and it is the practical maximum for a wood fence. Drop to 6 feet in exposed positions, with heavy solid boards, or on soft ground. Whatever the spacing, add one post at the end: it is the post everyone forgets.

Pickets: 2.2 per foot, not 2

This is the mistake that leaves people short. A nominal 1×6 picket is actually 5.5 inches wide, so 12 ÷ 5.5 = 2.2 pickets per foot. Dividing by 6 inches instead gives 200 pickets for a 100 foot fence — 19 short of the 219 you actually need.

Rails: 2, 3 or 4 per section

Fences under 5 feet tall need 2 rails per section. Five to 7 feet — the common 6 foot privacy fence — needs 3. An 8 foot fence needs 4, and 6×6 posts instead of 4×4.

Concrete: one bag per post

For a 4×4 post in a 10 to 12 inch hole, 24 inches deep, one 80 lb bag is the usual rule. Use the concrete calculator if your holes are a different size — it works out post holes exactly.

Common questions

How many fence posts do I need?

Divide the fence length by the post spacing — usually 8 feet — then add one post at the end.

How many pickets do I need for a 100 ft fence?

219, with 1×6 pickets. They are really 5.5 inches wide, so it is 2.2 pickets per foot, not 2.

How much concrete per fence post?

One 80 lb bag, for a 4×4 post in a 10 to 12 inch hole, 24 inches deep.
