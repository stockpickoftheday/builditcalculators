How the maths works

Board coverage: width plus gap

Each board covers its own width plus the gap beside it. A 5.5 inch board with a ⅛ inch gap covers 5.625 inches, so one square foot of deck takes 12 ÷ 5.625 = 2.13 linear feet of board. Narrower boards take more: a 3.5 inch board with the same gap needs 3.3 linear feet per square foot.

Joists: one every 16 inches, plus one

Joists run under the decking every 16 inches on center — that is the standard for 5/4 decking. Count them the same way as fence posts: length divided by spacing, plus one at the end. For a 16 foot deck that is 13 joists.

Screws: two per board at every joist

Each deck board gets two screws where it crosses each joist. That works out to about 350 screws per 100 square feet of deck — 3.5 per square foot. Buy stainless or coated deck screws; ordinary screws rust and stain the wood.

Do not forget the frame

This calculator covers decking, joists and screws. A real deck also needs beams, posts, footings, ledger board, flashing and railing. Footings and posts depend on your local frost depth and building code — check both before you build.

Common questions

How many deck screws do I need?

About 350 screws per 100 square feet of deck — two per board at every joist.

How far apart should deck joists be?

16 inches on center, which is the standard for 5/4 decking.

How many boards do I need for a deck?

It depends on board width. A 5.5 inch board with a ⅛ inch gap covers 5.625 inches, so one square foot of deck takes 2.13 linear feet of board.
