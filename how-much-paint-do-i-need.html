How-to guide

How much paint do I need?

A 12 by 12 foot room with 8 foot ceilings takes 2 gallons of paint for two coats on the walls — 3 gallons if you paint the ceiling too. One gallon covers about 350 square feet per coat, and almost every interior job needs two coats.

How the maths works

Step 1: the wall area

Add up all four wall lengths and multiply by the ceiling height. A 12 by 12 foot room with 8 foot ceilings has 384 square feet of wall.

Step 2: subtract doors and windows

Take off about 21 square feet per door (3 × 7 ft) and 15 per window (3 × 5 ft). One door and two windows takes the 12 by 12 room from 384 to 333 square feet.

Step 3: multiply by the coats, divide by 350

Two coats means 333 × 2 = 666 square feet of painting. At 350 square feet per gallon, that is 1.90 gallons — buy 2 gallons. Add the ceiling (144 sq ft, two coats) and it becomes 3 gallons.

How many coats?

Two, for almost every interior job. One coat rarely covers evenly — especially over a darker colour, new drywall, or a repair. Primer counts as a coat on bare drywall or stained walls, but it is not a substitute for the second coat of paint.

Is expensive paint worth it?

Usually yes. Premium paint covers in two coats where economy paint takes three or four, and lasts 10–15 years instead of 2–4. The dearer gallon is often the cheaper job.
