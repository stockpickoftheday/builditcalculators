How-to guide

How much topsoil or gravel do I need?

A 10 by 10 foot area, 6 inches deep, takes 1.85 cubic yards — 1.85 tons of topsoil or 2.59 tons of gravel. Suppliers sell topsoil by the cubic yard and gravel by the ton, so work out the volume first, then convert.

How the maths works

Cubic yards: length × width × depth, divided by 27

A 10 by 10 foot area at 6 inches deep is 10 × 10 × (6 ÷ 12) = 50 cubic feet, and 50 ÷ 27 = 1.85 cubic yards.

Tons: multiply by the weight per yard

Topsoil weighs about 1 ton per cubic yard, gravel about 1.4 tons, and sand about 1.35 tons. So 1.85 cubic yards is 1.85 tons of topsoil, 2.59 tons of gravel, or 2.50 tons of sand.

Which material?

Topsoil for lawns and garden beds. Crusher run (road base) for driveways and paths — it packs down hard, and it is the cheapest gravel at $20–$40 a ton delivered. Pea gravel for paths and drainage at $30–$60 a ton. For vegetables, screened loam with compost mixed in; blueberries are the exception — they need acidic soil, not ordinary topsoil.

Common questions

How many tons of gravel are in a cubic yard?

About 1.4 tons.

Which gravel is cheapest?

Crusher run (road base), about $20–$40 a ton delivered. Pea gravel costs the most, about $30–$60 a ton.

What soil is best for vegetables?

Screened loam with compost mixed in. Blueberries are the exception — they need acidic soil, not ordinary topsoil.
