How the maths works

Volume first, weight second

Volume is the same for every material: area in square feet times depth in inches, divided by 324, gives cubic yards. Then weight is cubic yards times the material's weight per yard. That is where materials differ — a yard of mulch weighs about 500 lb, a yard of gravel about 2,800 lb.

Wet material weighs more — wet topsoil can reach 2,800 to 3,200 lb per yard. If you are ordering by the ton or hauling it yourself, ask your supplier what their material weighs. Their scale ticket wins over any calculator.

How deep?

Six inches of topsoil is a good depth for a new lawn or a garden bed. For top-dressing an existing lawn, 1 to 2 inches is enough. For a gravel driveway or path, 3 to 4 inches of compacted gravel holds up well.

Three grades of gravel — and what each costs

Gravel is sold by the ton, and the grade decides both the price and the job it suits. Prices are per ton delivered, typical US prices for 2026 — they vary a lot by region, and delivery often adds $50 to $150 a trip.

GradeSizeBest forPrice per ton
Crusher run / road base (#21A)¾ in down to dustDriveway base — the fines compact into a hard surface$20–$40
#57 crushed stone¾ in, angular edgesDrainage, driveways, concrete mix$25–$50
Pea gravel⅜ in, roundedPaths, patios, decoration — smooth underfoot$30–$60

Crusher run is the cheapest because it is just crushed rock with the dust left in — and that dust is exactly what makes it lock together. Pea gravel costs the most because it is screened, washed and rounded. Do not use pea gravel for a driveway: the round stones never lock together, so they shift under tires. One ton is roughly 0.7 cubic yards, so the calculator's cubic yards times 1.4 gives you the tons to order.

Types of topsoil — and which plants each suits

"Topsoil" on a delivery ticket can mean several different soils. What it is made of decides what will grow well in it.

Soil typeWhat it is likeBest plants
LoamA balanced mix of sand, silt and clay — the gardener's favouriteAlmost everything: most vegetables, lawns, roses, shrubs
SandyDrains fast, warms up early, low in nutrientsCarrots, radishes, potatoes, rosemary, thyme, lavender
ClayHeavy and sticky, rich in nutrients, holds waterCabbage, broccoli, kale, beans, roses, hydrangeas
SiltySmooth and fertile, holds moisture wellTomatoes, peppers, leafy greens, peonies, hostas
Peaty (acidic)Dark, spongy, low pH (4.5–5.5)Blueberries, rhododendrons, azaleas, camellias, heather
Chalky (alkaline)Pale, free-draining, high pH (7.5–8.5)Lavender, clematis, lilac, spinach, cabbage, kale

If you are buying topsoil for a vegetable garden, ask for screened loam with compost mixed in — that is the safest all-rounder. And if you want blueberries, do not plant them in ordinary topsoil: they need acidic soil, and in heavy clay they will barely fruit. Mix in peat or use an ericaceous (acid-loving) compost instead.

Common questions

How many tons of gravel are in a cubic yard?

About 1.4 tons.

Which gravel is cheapest?

Crusher run (road base), about $20–$40 a ton delivered. Pea gravel costs the most, about $30–$60 a ton.

What soil is best for vegetables?

Screened loam with compost mixed in. Blueberries are the exception — they need acidic soil, not ordinary topsoil.
