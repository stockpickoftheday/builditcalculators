How the maths works

Volume first, weight second

Volume is the same for every material: area in square feet times depth in inches, divided by 324, gives cubic yards. Then weight is cubic yards times the material's weight per yard. That is where materials differ — a yard of mulch weighs about 500 lb, a yard of gravel about 2,800 lb.

Wet material weighs more — wet topsoil can reach 2,800 to 3,200 lb per yard. If you are ordering by the ton or hauling it yourself, ask your supplier what their material weighs. Their scale ticket wins over any calculator.

How deep?

Six inches of topsoil is a good depth for a new lawn or a garden bed. For top-dressing an existing lawn, 1 to 2 inches is enough. For a gravel driveway or path, 3 to 4 inches of compacted gravel holds up well.

Figures verified November 2026 against The Calculator Site, US Army FM 5-434 material weights and several yard calculators. Weights are dry planning figures and vary with moisture and compaction. This page is an estimate for planning only.
