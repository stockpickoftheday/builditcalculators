How the maths works

Sheets: area divided by 32, plus waste

Wall area is the room's perimeter times its height, minus doors and windows. Add the ceiling if you are boarding it too. Divide by the sheet size — 32 square feet for a 4 by 8 — and add 10% for cuts around outlets, corners and angles.

Finishing materials come per sheet

The estimating guides from the gypsum manufacturers work out to about the same numbers on every calculator: 32 screws per wall sheet (about one per square foot), 1 gallon of joint compound per 100 square feet for a three-coat finish, and 0.37 feet of paper tape per square foot of board. Ceilings take about half again as many screws as walls, because gravity is working against the board.

One pound of screws goes a long way

A 1 lb box of 1¼ inch drywall screws holds roughly 200 screws — about 6 sheets' worth. A 5 lb box covers about 30 sheets. Buy screws by the box, not by the handful.

Common questions

How many drywall sheets do I need for a room?

Take the wall area (perimeter times height, minus doors and windows), divide by 32 square feet per 4×8 sheet, and add 10% for cuts.

How many screws per sheet of drywall?

About 32 per wall sheet. Ceilings take about half again as many, because gravity works against the board.

How much joint compound do I need?

About 1 gallon per 100 square feet for a three-coat finish.
