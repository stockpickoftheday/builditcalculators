How to measure a project

Measure twice, in feet

Every calculator on this site works in feet and inches. Measure the longest side first, then the other side, and write both down before you start typing. For a room, measure each wall at floor level — walls are rarely as square as they look, so take the biggest number if the two ends differ.

Depth matters as much as area

A patio is not just 10 by 10 feet — it is 10 by 10 feet at 4 inches thick. The same area at 6 inches takes half as much material again. The calculators ask for depth wherever it matters: concrete thickness, mulch depth, topsoil depth.

Always add a little extra

Every calculator here adds a waste allowance by default — usually 10%. It covers spillage, uneven ground, cut-offs and the odd mistake. Running out halfway through a pour or a paint job costs far more than one extra bag or gallon.

When the numbers get big, call a truck

Once a concrete job passes about one cubic yard — 45 bags of 80 lb mix — ready-mix delivered by truck is usually cheaper and far less work than mixing bags by hand. The concrete calculator tells you when you have crossed that line.

These calculators are estimates for planning only. They are not professional advice, and a supplier's own figures win over anything here. Confirm quantities with your supplier before you order, and check your local building code before you build anything structural.
