How the maths works

Area, then add waste

Room area is length times width. Add 10% for a straight lay — it covers the cuts at the walls, a plank or two that arrives damaged, and the odd mistake. Go to 15% for diagonal layouts or rooms with lots of corners and closets, and 20% for herringbone or chevron.

Boxes: divide and round up

Flooring is sold by the box, not by the square foot, so divide what you need to order by the coverage on the box and round up. Coverage varies by brand — laminate is usually about 20 square feet a box, vinyl plank about 24, hardwood about 20 — so check the label and type the exact number in.

Underlayment and acclimation

Laminate and floating hardwood need underlayment between the planks and the subfloor — it softens footsteps and blocks moisture. Rolls usually cover 100 square feet. Vinyl plank with an attached pad often needs none. Let wood flooring sit in the room for at least 48 hours before you lay it — solid hardwood up to 7 days — so it adjusts to your home's temperature and humidity.

Common questions

How much flooring do I need for a 12×12 room?

144 square feet, and 159 with 10% waste — 8 boxes of laminate at 20 square feet a box.

How much extra flooring should I buy?

10% for a straight lay in a rectangular room. Use 15% for diagonal layouts or rooms with lots of corners, and 20% for herringbone.

How many square feet are in a box of flooring?

It varies by brand — laminate is usually about 20, vinyl plank about 24, hardwood about 20, and engineered hardwood about 22. The exact number is printed on the box.
