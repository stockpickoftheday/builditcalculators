How much mulch do I need?

A 10 by 20 foot bed, 3 inches deep, takes 1.85 cubic yards of mulch — 25 bags of 2 cubic feet. The maths is length × width × depth, divided by 27 to get cubic yards. One cubic yard covers 108 square feet at 3 inches deep.

How the maths works

Cubic yards: length × width × depth, divided by 27

A cubic yard is 27 cubic feet. A 10 by 20 foot bed at 3 inches deep is 10 × 20 × (3 ÷ 12) = 50 cubic feet, and 50 ÷ 27 = 1.85 cubic yards.

Bags: divide by what one bag holds

A 2 cubic foot bag holds 2 cubic feet, so 50 cubic feet ÷ 2 = 25 bags. One cubic yard is 13.5 bags of 2 cubic feet.

How deep?

Two to three inches for most flower and shrub beds, four inches around trees. More than 4 inches can suffocate roots — and mulch piled against a trunk rots the bark, so keep it a few inches clear of the trunk.

Bulk or bags?

Bags make sense up to about a cubic yard — 13 or 14 bags. Past that, bulk mulch delivered by the yard is usually much cheaper per cubic yard, and you skip hauling dozens of bags home.

Common questions

How much mulch do I need for a 100 sq ft bed?

At 3 inches deep, about 0.93 cubic yards — around 13 bags of 2 cubic feet.

How deep should mulch be?

Two to three inches for most flower and shrub beds, four inches around trees. More than 4 inches can suffocate roots.

What mulch is best for a hot, dry garden?

Light-coloured bark or straw, 3 to 4 inches deep. It holds moisture and keeps roots cool; gravel can get hot enough to scorch roots.
