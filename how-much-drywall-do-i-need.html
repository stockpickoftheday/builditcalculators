How-to guide

How much drywall do I need?

A 12 by 12 foot room with 8 foot ceilings takes 17 sheets of 4×8 drywall — plus 544 screws, 5 gallons of joint compound and 176 feet of paper tape. The maths is just area: work out the wall area, add the ceiling if you are boarding it, and divide by 32 square feet per sheet.

How the maths works

Wall area: perimeter × height, minus doors and windows

Add up all four wall lengths, multiply by the ceiling height, then subtract about 21 sq ft for each door and 15 sq ft for each window. A 12 by 12 foot room with 8 foot ceilings has 384 sq ft of wall — 333 sq ft after one door and two windows.

Sheets: divide by 32, add 10%

A 4×8 sheet covers 32 sq ft. Divide your total area by 32 and add 10% for cuts. The 12 by 12 room needs 477 sq ft of board with the ceiling — 477 ÷ 32 = 14.9, plus 10% is 16.4, so 17 sheets.

Screws: about 32 per sheet

Budget about 32 screws per wall sheet. Ceilings take about half again as many, because gravity works against the board.

Joint compound and tape

Allow about 1 gallon of joint compound per 100 sq ft for a three-coat finish, and enough paper tape to cover every seam — 176 feet for the 12 by 12 room.

Cutting waste down

Hang sheets horizontally on walls — fewer seams to tape, and the long edges land on studs. Measure twice before you cut: a sheet cut short is the most common waste on a drywall job.
