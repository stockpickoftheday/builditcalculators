How long will a power station last?

How the maths works

Usable energy: the label minus 15%

A station's watt-hour rating is measured at the battery's own voltage. Running 120 V devices through the inverter loses about 15%, so the energy you can actually use is the label times 0.85. A 1024 Wh station gives 1024 × 0.85 = 870 Wh.

Daily use: watts × hours, added up

Each device uses its running watts times the hours it runs each day. Phone 10 × 3 = 30 Wh, laptop 60 × 4 = 240 Wh, router 10 × 24 = 240 Wh, light 10 × 5 = 50 Wh, fan 50 × 8 = 400 Wh. Add them up: 30 + 240 + 240 + 50 + 400 = 960 Wh per day.

Days of backup: usable ÷ daily

Divide the usable energy by the daily use. 870 ÷ 960 = 0.9 days — about 22 hours. If the answer is under a day, your load is too big for the station, or you need a bigger station.

Station size needed: daily × days ÷ 0.85

Work backwards from the load: daily use times the days you want, divided by 0.85 to cover the inverter loss. 960 × 1 ÷ 0.85 = 1130 Wh for one full day on the example load.

Cold weather: take off another 20%

Lithium batteries give up about 20% of their usable capacity below freezing, and most stations will not charge at all below 0°C (32°F). On the example load, a 1024 Wh station in the cold gives 870 × 0.8 = 696 Wh, which lasts 0.7 days instead of 0.9. Keep the station somewhere warm — inside, in the car, or in an insulated bag — and charge it indoors.

Common questions

How long will a 1000 Wh power station run a fridge?

About 5 to 6 hours running flat out. A 1000 Wh station gives about 850 Wh of usable energy, and a full-size fridge draws about 150 watts while it runs, so 850 ÷ 150 is about 5.7 hours. In real use a fridge cycles on and off, so it stretches to most of a day.

Why does my power station run out faster than the label says?

Three reasons. First, the inverter takes about 15% — a 1000 Wh station gives about 850 Wh. Second, the Wh on the label is measured at the battery's own voltage; running 120 V devices through the inverter loses a little more. Third, cold weather cuts capacity by about 20% below freezing. The calculator builds the first and third in.

What size power station do I need for a day without power?

Add up what you would run: watts × hours for each device. Divide the total by 0.85. The example load on this page — phone, laptop, router, light and fan — uses 960 Wh a day, so it needs a station of about 1130 Wh for one full day.

Can I charge it while I use it?

Most stations allow pass-through charging — you can plug them into the wall or a solar panel while they power your devices. Charging is slower while the station is also running devices, and a few small stations pause charging when the load is heavy. Check your station's manual.
